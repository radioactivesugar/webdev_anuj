REVENUE GROWTH

At RBI-licensed NBFCs, 600% annual interest is board-approved, "reasonable," and perfectly legal.

09/04/2026 Duration: 1 Week Writer: Mrunmayee Kulkarni Information Design, Web Development eCharts, iFrame, CSS, JavaScript, Figma, GitHub, Google Gemini, Claude Code Live link: Intermission/Revenue Growth

"The very first application came back sanctioned by an RBI-licensed NBFC, at an annualised interest rate of 886%."

The story examined a cluster of Non-Banking Financial Companies (NBFCs) running high-APR personal loan products, whose revenues rose sharply through FY25 — and asked what that rapid growth said about the lending practices behind it.

DATA

The data provided was broken down into 2 layers of information to make it digestible for the readers.

Primary Layer
of data

Legible without any interaction.

The shift in company valuation/revenue

Comparative scale of growth across companies

The overall magnitude of the trend

Secondary Layer
of data

revealed on hover

Company Name

Actual revenue figures

Translation of the growth multiple

Year of establishment

DESIGN

The result is a packed bubble chart, titled Class of FY25, clustering NBFCs whose high-APR personal loan revenues grew sharply over the past year:

Bubble size maps to how many times a company's revenue multiplied between FY24 and FY25

Bubble colour distinguishes profitable companies (a green gradient, scaling with growth multiple) from loss-making ones (flat orange/red)

Hover state — surfaces company-level detail

The revenue in FY24 and FY25, growth multiple, year of establishment, and the loan brands operating under each NBFC.

A minimum size cap was applied to the bubbles. Without it, companies with 1–2x growth would be visually negligible next to outliers growing 6,000x+. The cap was a deliberate accessibility trade-off — sacrificing strict proportionality so that every company in the dataset remains visible and comparable.

The final visualisation was embedded into the story via an iframe, and published on 16th April.
